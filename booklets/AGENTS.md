# Agent Instructions — Books and Booklets

These instructions apply to every book and booklet under this directory, including multilingual editions and multi-volume works. They supplement the repository README and Papyrxis Workspace documentation.

## Mission

Build a coherent learning or reading experience, not merely a sequence of chapters. Each booklet should have a defined audience, scope, learning path, and honest statement of what is complete.

Before writing, determine:
- the reader's prior knowledge;
- the specific concepts the booklet will establish;
- the order in which those concepts depend on one another;
- the examples, proofs, exercises, or diagrams needed;
- what is finished, provisional, outlined, or intentionally deferred.

A booklet may be a work in progress. Its status must be visible to the reader rather than hidden behind polished front matter.

## Repository boundaries

- This directory owns booklet content, chapter structure, language editions, figures, references, and booklet-specific metadata.
- Shared templates, reusable LaTeX components, build scripts, export tooling, and common automation belong in Papyrxis Workspace.
- Read the root README and docs/REPOSITORY-BOUNDARIES.md before changing layout or tooling.
- Do not duplicate Workspace components or introduce a second build system.
- Do not edit generated files under build/. Edit source files and rebuild.
- Do not put a personal CV, roadmap, or unrelated article into a booklet directory.

## Inspect before editing

1. Read the booklet's README, main.tex, metadata, front matter, chapter index, and back matter.
2. Read workspace.yml and the shared template/preset that the booklet uses.
3. Inspect the English and Persian entry points before changing shared structure.
4. Check the existing chapter status and references. Preserve intentional outlines and unfinished sections.
5. Keep changes scoped to the named booklet unless a shared template change is explicitly required.

## Structure and identity

Create a new booklet using the existing scaffold, for example:

    make new-booklet SLUG=15-new-topic

Follow the established numbered-slug convention. A booklet normally has:
- a stable slug and publication title;
- a separate entry point and metadata for each language;
- front matter such as title, preface, and reading guidance;
- ordered chapter source files;
- back matter for notes, references, appendices, or an index.

Use the layout generated by the project tooling. Do not rename a booklet or its public output identity casually; filenames, references, build configuration, and published artifacts may depend on it.

Each booklet must make sense as an independent work. A series may share conventions and references, but it should not require the reader to have downloaded every other booklet to understand the current one.

## Learning design and chapter writing

- Begin each chapter with a clear purpose and connect it to what the reader already knows.
- Introduce concepts before relying on them in proofs or examples.
- Build from concrete examples to general statements where that helps understanding.
- State assumptions and definitions explicitly.
- For mathematical material, distinguish intuition, formal definition, lemma, theorem, proof, and example.
- Check proofs for hidden assumptions, circular reasoning, undefined notation, and missing cases.
- For systems and programming material, explain the model at the right abstraction level and identify platform or implementation assumptions.
- Use diagrams, tables, and code examples only when they make a concept easier to understand.
- Add exercises only when their expected outcome and prerequisites are clear.
- Avoid padding, repeated introductions, and unsupported historical claims.

## Honest progress and outlines

- Mark an outline as an outline. A chapter that contains only a title, a summary, or a list of future topics is not a completed chapter.
- Do not silently turn a placeholder into polished prose without resolving the underlying content.
- Preserve explicit progress notes where they help readers distinguish finished material from planned material.
- Do not describe an ongoing series as a finished textbook or complete course.
- In a project such as Mathesis, which deliberately records learning from first principles and includes outlines, retain that editorial transparency.
- If a chapter depends on material not yet written, state that dependency plainly.

## English and Persian editions

- Keep language-specific source files and metadata separate.
- Translate meaning, notation, examples, and pedagogical intent; do not mechanically translate sentence structure.
- Keep symbols, variable names, code, identifiers, and technical terms stable across editions unless a documented localization choice is necessary.
- Use the project's glossary for recurring terminology.
- Check right-to-left punctuation, mixed Persian/Latin text, equation direction, cross-references, and page breaks.
- Do not claim the Persian edition is complete when substantive chapters are still placeholders or links to the English text.
- Keep the two editions aligned in chapter order and conceptual scope.

Respect the language engines configured in workspace.yml. Never switch TeX engines globally to solve a problem isolated to one language edition.

## Front matter, references, and rights

- Ensure the title page, author, edition, language, version, and publication metadata agree.
- Use a preface or introduction to explain scope, prerequisites, and how to read the work.
- Keep bibliography entries accurate and traceable. Verify author, title, edition, year, publisher, and stable URL or DOI.
- Attribute borrowed diagrams, examples, datasets, and quotations.
- Preserve the license selected for the booklet and its copyright notices.
- Do not reuse figures, cover artwork, or third-party text without checking the applicable license.
- Keep references relevant to the chapter where they are cited.

## Typesetting and build quality

Use the supported commands:
- make booklet-list to inspect booklets;
- make booklet SLUG=<slug> LANG=en|fa to build a language edition;
- make booklet-all or make build for all configured booklets;
- make booklet-watch SLUG=<slug> LANG=en|fa while editing;
- make booklet-clean to remove generated output.

Before declaring a booklet ready:
- build each language that is claimed to be complete;
- inspect the resulting PDF page by page;
- check table of contents, headings, references, equations, figures, fonts, RTL layout, widow/orphan lines, and blank pages;
- verify every internal and external link;
- ensure the title, author, language, and copyright metadata are correct;
- confirm generated files remain under build/ and are not hand-edited.

A successful TeX exit code is necessary but not sufficient. Do not claim a build or visual review that was not actually performed.

## Completion criteria

A booklet is ready for publication when its intended audience and scope are clear, its chapter sequence is coherent, its completeness status is honest, its content and references are accurate, its language editions are consistent, its license and metadata are correct, and its output PDFs have been inspected.
